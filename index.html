The robot stays stiff in the plane of motion while remaining compliant along the contact normal. The predicted virtual displacement orients the compliance frame, so the compliant axis follows the contact direction as it changes, as in the Pivot task.

Force Modulation

Lowering stiffness alone cannot press on a surface. CompVLA shifts the equilibrium pose along the contact direction, turning the impedance spring into a controllable contact force while keeping interaction forces within safe limits, as in the Erase Whiteboard task.

Abstract

Contact-rich manipulation, requiring robots to regulate not only motion but also how they yield to external forces, has emerged as the next frontier for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. However, existing VLAs output purely kinematic commands, degrading performance on real-world contact-rich tasks. In this paper, we introduce CompVLA, a unified VLA framework that jointly predicts motion and stiffness matrix from RGB and language inputs. Our approach augments the conventional architecture with a dedicated Compliance Expert, which outputs time-varying stiffness and virtual displacement profiles executed via geometric impedance control. We demonstrate that CompVLA achieves the highest average success rate across diverse contact-rich tasks, outperforming both vanilla and compliance-aware VLA baselines, with ablations confirming each component is essential.

Why Compliance for VLAs?

Most VLAs execute trajectories through position control and do not model the force exchanges that arise during contact. In tasks such as polishing, peg-in-hole assembly, and whiteboard erasing, small tracking errors can make the robot lose contact or apply excessive force.

Compliance control specifies the end-effector's closed-loop behavior as a virtual mass–spring–damper, $$ \Lambda_d \dot{\mathcal V}_e + D_d \mathcal V_e + K_d X_e = \mathcal F_{\text{ext}}, $$ where \( X_e \) and \( \mathcal V_e \) are the body-frame pose and twist errors on SE(3). In the quasistatic regime this reduces to \( K_d X_e = \mathcal F_{\text{ext}} \), so the stiffness \( K_d \) largely governs the force–displacement relationship. We realize this behavior with geometric impedance control (GIC), which is coordinate-free and almost-globally exponentially stable.

Compliance, however, is not always the answer: a stiff controller tracks free-space motion more accurately. A task needs variable compliance that stays stiff for precise tracking and yields on contact. Hand-tuning such profiles does not scale, so CompVLA learns them from demonstrations.

CompVLA: Motion, Stiffness, and Virtual Displacement

Compliance-aware Action Representation

At each timestep the policy predicts a motion chunk \( \mathcal M_t = (m_t, \dots, m_{t+T-1}) \), where each token is $$ m_t = (\mathcal V_t,\, g_t,\, k_t,\, d_t). $$ \( \mathcal V_t \in \mathbb R^6 \) is the reference TCP twist and \( g_t \) the gripper command. The diagonal stiffness \( k_t = [k^R_t; k^p_t] \in \mathbb R^6 \) sets how the robot yields along each axis, and the virtual displacement \( d_t = [d^R_t; d^p_t] \in \mathbb R^6 \) both orients the compliance frame (directional compliance) and shifts the target pose to generate a contact force (force modulation).

Dual-expert Architecture

CompVLA builds on a \( \pi_0 \) backbone (PaliGemma VLM) with two experts attached through cross-attention. The Action Expert predicts the reference TCP twist and gripper command. Its integrated trajectory conditions the Compliance Expert, which predicts \( k_t \) and \( d_t \). Both experts are trained jointly with flow matching, \( \mathcal L = \mathcal L_a + \mathcal L_c \).

Overall framework of CompVLA and the geometric impedance controller.

Learning Compliance from Demonstration

Stiffness and virtual displacement targets are inferred from force/torque recorded during force-feedback teleoperation. The compliance frame's first axis is aligned with the measured contact direction; along it the stiffness is set from the force magnitude by piecewise-linear interpolation, $$ k_{\text{low}}(|f_t|) = \begin{cases} k_{\max}, & |f_t| < f_{\min} \\[2pt] k_{\max} - (k_{\max}-k_{\min})\,\dfrac{|f_t|-f_{\min}}{f_{\max}-f_{\min}}, & f_{\min} \le |f_t| \le f_{\max} \\[6pt] k_{\min}, & |f_t| > f_{\max} \end{cases} $$ while the orthogonal axes keep \( k_{\max} \). The displacement magnitude is the demonstrated force divided by \( k_{\text{low}} \), giving \( K_0 = \mathrm{diag}(k_{\text{low}}, k_{\max}, k_{\max}) \) and \( d_t \) along the contact direction.

Compliant Motion Generation with GIC

The reference pose is deformed by \( d_t \) into a virtual target \( R^v = R\,\mathrm{Exp}(d^R),\; p^v = p + R\,d^p \), and the diagonal stiffness is mapped into the TCP frame through the compliance-frame rotations \( \tilde R^R, \tilde R^p \): $$ K = \begin{bmatrix} \tilde R^R \mathrm{diag}(k^R)(\tilde R^R)^\top & 0 \\ 0 & \tilde R^p \mathrm{diag}(k^p)(\tilde R^p)^\top \end{bmatrix}. $$ The task-space wrench \( \mathcal F = -K X_e - C \mathcal V_e \) is mapped to joint torques by \( \tau = J^\top(\theta)\mathcal F \) at high frequency, coupling low-frequency policy outputs with a high-frequency interaction control loop.

Real-world Experiments

We evaluate on a 7-DoF Franka Research 3 with three RealSense cameras. Force/torque readings are recorded for training labels only and are not used by the policy at evaluation time. Three contact-rich tasks span the regimes where compliance matters: surface tracking under pose uncertainty (Erase Whiteboard), continuously varying compliance direction (Pivot), and force-regulated pressing (Cup Dispense). A Pick and Place task checks that standard manipulation is preserved.

Contact-rich Demonstration Dataset

We release the demonstrations used to train CompVLA: 100 episodes per task, collected with a force-feedback leader–follower teleoperation system. Each episode contains three camera views, joint positions, joint torques, and wrist force/torque readings, synchronized at 10 Hz and stored in the LeRobot format.
